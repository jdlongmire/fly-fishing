# Green Machine Stonefly

**Type:** Nymph (beadhead, scud hook)
**Imitates:** Stonefly nymph
**Sizes I carry:** Built on the Green Machine chassis (barbed scud hooks, 18, 20, 22)
**Colors:** Olive D-Rib body over flash, black bead, barred black and white flash appendages

## When it produces

Not yet recorded. First tied Nov 26, 2026 and not yet fished. Stonefly nymphs live in the drift year-round, so the imitation is a fair bet whenever trout are feeding on the bottom.

## How to fish it

Dead drift it deep on a tight line or under an indicator, like other stonefly nymphs. (Standard practice for the type; JD's own results are not yet logged.)

## Tying notes

JD's stonefly built on the Green Machine chassis, first tied Nov 26, 2026. The idea: the Green Machine body already gives a segmented, flashy abdomen; add the two appendage pairs that make the stonefly silhouette.

- Hook: Scud hook, barbed (Green Machine chassis)
- Bead: Black
- Body: Olive D-Rib over a flash underbody, same build as the Green Machine
- Thorax: JD's custom dub mix, picked out buggy
- Tails: Two strands of barred black and white Krystal Flash at the bend
- Antennae: Two strands of the same barred flash at the head

The identification is in the counts: stonefly nymphs carry exactly two tails and two antennae. Mayflies and damselflies carry three tails, so the two-and-two layout is what makes this read stonefly.

Trim lesson from the first version: the first tie left both pairs three to four times body length, which read as the right blueprint drawn large. Trimmed to about body length, the imitation tightens up. The antennae can be left a touch long on purpose; they are the part a drifting nymph waves, and the extra length buys movement.
